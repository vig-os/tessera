# [PR 496](https://github.com/vig-os/tessera/pull/496) ci(nix-check): raise the leg timeout to 120m, the spread outgrew 90

#461's x86_64 leg was killed at **89 minutes** having done nothing wrong. The 90-minute cap is now
cutting real work rather than catching hangs.

## Measured

Same workflow, all GitHub-hosted runners:

| commit | x86_64 | aarch64 |
|---|---|---|
| 7d2eaf8d | 43m success | 41m success |
| 33aa5134 | 46m success | 43m success |
| vortex pin PR | 57m success | 43m success |
| 1442ba12 (#461) | **89m TIMEOUT** | 54m success |

## The axis is variance, not architecture

Worth being exact, because the single-sample reading is tempting and was proposed: from the 89m/54m run
alone it looks like *"x86_64 is ~1.7× slower than aarch64"*. But on the two runs above it, x86 is within
**5–7%** of aarch64.

- x86_64's own range: **43–89m, 2.1× spread**
- aarch64's own range: **41–54m, 1.3× spread**

There is no architectural constant to tune for. There is a wide distribution on one arch whose upper
tail now crosses the cap — and that leads to a different fix (headroom, plus finding the tail's cause)
than "x86 is slower" would (rebalancing work between arches).

## Why 120

The worst observed draw plus ~35%, following the principle the existing comment already states: *set
the cap above the observed spread, not just above the median.* Deliberately not higher — the cap still
has to fail a genuinely hung leg in reasonable time, and GitHub's ceiling is 6 hours.

## What this is not

**This raises the ceiling; it does not make anything faster.** The variance is the real defect, tracked
in #495. The sampler from #488 now records memory, disk and load average every 30s on every leg, printed
into the streamed step log so it survives a kill — so the next long draw can be *attributed* (memory
pressure / I/O starvation / slower silicon) rather than guessed at. Baseline from #488's green legs:
peak 10.2 GB (x86) and 11.6 GB (aarch64) of 16 GB, with 74–92 GB disk free throughout.

It is also a **hard prerequisite** for the `--max-jobs` work under #483: at 89 of 90 minutes, any change
trading wall-clock for peak footprint converts intermittent failures into reliable ones. That is why the
concurrency cap has not landed and should not land before this.

One contributing factor I should name, since it is mine: the vortex fork pin (#480) makes ~27 vortex
crates compile from a git source with no binary-cache substituter where they were previously downloaded.
That raised compile load on every leg. It was the right call for #468/#472 and it is temporary, but it is
part of why the tail moved now rather than earlier.
